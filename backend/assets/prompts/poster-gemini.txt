Your task is to transform the provided product image and product information into a **premium, commercially effective marketplace infographic**.

The final image must look like it was designed by a professional international e-commerce design team — NOT like a generic AI template.

0. MOST IMPORTANT RULE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

DO NOT START DESIGNING IMMEDIATELY.

FIRST:

1. Analyze the product.
2. Identify its category.
3. Identify its target buyer.
4. Identify its strongest selling proposition.
5. Identify the 2–4 most important secondary benefits.
6. Analyze the product's shape, proportions, materials, colors and visual character.
7. Decide what composition best communicates THIS specific product.
8. Decide where every major element should be positioned.
9. Only then generate the final image.

DO NOT use a generic infographic template.

The layout must be created specifically for this product.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. PRODUCT = PRIMARY SOURCE OF TRUTH
   ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The supplied product image is the absolute visual reference.

Preserve the product accurately.

DO NOT:

* change its shape;
* change proportions;
* change dimensions;
* redesign the product;
* invent components;
* invent buttons;
* invent ports;
* invent materials;
* invent textures;
* invent functions;
* change logos;
* change brand marks;
* change product colors;
* remove important physical details;
* create impossible geometry;
* merge separate components incorrectly.

The product must remain immediately recognizable as the exact original product.

You MAY improve:

* lighting;
* shadows;
* reflections;
* perspective;
* positioning;
* background;
* environment;
* depth;
* presentation quality.

A beautiful but inaccurate product is a FAILURE.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
2. CANVAS & SAFE AREAS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Treat the image as a structured graphic design canvas.

Use a clean invisible grid.

Divide the canvas into:

* top zone;
* central visual zone;
* supporting information zone;
* bottom breathing zone.

Keep all important text and information inside a safe area.

DO NOT place text:

* directly on top of important product details;
* too close to the image edge;
* over visually complex areas;
* where contrast is insufficient.

Maintain generous margins.

Never allow typography or icons to touch the canvas edges.

The composition must feel intentionally aligned rather than randomly positioned.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
3. VISUAL HIERARCHY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The viewer must understand the image in approximately this order:

1. WHAT IS THE PRODUCT?
2. WHAT IS ITS MAIN BENEFIT?
3. WHY IS IT BETTER / USEFUL?
4. WHAT ARE ITS IMPORTANT FEATURES?
5. WHAT ARE THE TECHNICAL DETAILS?

Use exactly this hierarchy unless the product strongly requires another structure.

The visual hierarchy must be obvious even if the viewer only looks at the image for one second.

Hierarchy is created through:

* scale;
* position;
* contrast;
* font weight;
* whitespace;
* color;
* depth;
* visual emphasis.

Do NOT make every element equally large or equally bright.

One element must clearly dominate.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
4. PRODUCT POSITIONING
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The product is the HERO of the composition.

Position it deliberately.

Preferred positioning:

CENTER:
Use when the product itself is the strongest visual selling point.

LEFT:
Use when text or feature explanations need to occupy the right side.

RIGHT:
Use when the main headline and benefits need to occupy the left side.

DIAGONAL / DYNAMIC:
Use for sports, tools, technology or products that benefit from energy and movement.

BOTTOM-CENTER:
Use when creating a premium hero composition with typography above.

MULTI-PRODUCT:
Arrange products in a clear hierarchy:
PRIMARY PRODUCT = largest
SECONDARY PRODUCTS = smaller
ACCESSORIES = supporting scale

Never place the product randomly.

The product should have enough breathing room around it.

Do not crop the product unless intentional cropping clearly improves the composition.

Avoid placing the product directly against text.

Use visual separation between product and typography.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
5. TEXT POSITIONING
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Typography must have a deliberate relationship with the product.

NEVER scatter text around the canvas.

Use one clear text zone.

Preferred structures:

OPTION A:
HEADLINE → above / left
PRODUCT → center / right
BENEFITS → below / opposite side

OPTION B:
PRODUCT → center
HEADLINE → top
BENEFITS → bottom

OPTION C:
PRODUCT → left
HEADLINE → right
FEATURES → right underneath headline

OPTION D:
PRODUCT → dominant center
MINIMAL TEXT → surrounding negative space

Choose ONE primary structure and execute it consistently.

Do not mix multiple unrelated layouts.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
6. TEXT SIZE RATIO
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Use a clear typography scale.

MAIN HEADLINE:
approximately 100% visual importance.

SUBHEADLINE:
approximately 50–65% of headline size.

FEATURE LABEL:
approximately 35–50% of headline size.

SECONDARY INFORMATION:
approximately 25–35% of headline size.

Do NOT make secondary information as large as the main selling proposition.

The headline must be immediately readable.

Supporting text must be readable without competing with the product.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
7. TEXT LENGTH
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Use the minimum amount of text required to communicate the idea.

Prefer:

"Мощность 1200 Вт"

over:

"Высокая невероятная мощность устройства составляет целых 1200 ватт для эффективного использования"

Prefer:

"До 8 часов работы"

over:

"Устройство способно работать в течение продолжительного времени до 8 часов"

Short text = stronger visual hierarchy.

Never fill empty space with unnecessary text.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
8. TEXT LANGUAGE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

ALL VISIBLE TEXT MUST BE IN RUSSIAN.

Do not translate Russian product information into English.

Russian must be:

* natural;
* grammatically correct;
* concise;
* commercially appropriate;
* easy to understand.

NEVER generate:

* pseudo-text;
* random letters;
* meaningless symbols;
* distorted Cyrillic;
* fake words;
* incorrect numbers;
* invented specifications.

If exact text cannot be rendered correctly, simplify the wording rather than generating corrupted text.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
9. TYPOGRAPHY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Use professional modern typography.

Preferred typefaces / visual styles:

* modern grotesk;
* neo-grotesk;
* geometric sans-serif;
* contemporary editorial sans-serif;
* clean premium sans-serif.

Good visual references include the characteristics of:

* Inter;
* SF Pro;
* Helvetica Neue;
* Manrope;
* Neue Haas Grotesk;
* similar modern professional sans-serif systems.

Do NOT imitate a specific brand.

Use maximum TWO font families.

Prefer one family with multiple weights.

Recommended hierarchy:

HEADLINE:
Bold / ExtraBold

SUBHEADLINE:
Semibold / Medium

BODY:
Regular / Medium

SMALL LABEL:
Medium

Avoid:

* decorative fonts;
* childish fonts unless the product requires them;
* random futuristic fonts;
* excessive italics;
* excessive outlines;
* excessive shadows;
* 3D text unless genuinely appropriate;
* glowing text everywhere;
* overly condensed text;
* stretched text.

Typography must feel expensive and intentional.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
10. TYPOGRAPHIC ALIGNMENT
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Choose ONE primary alignment:

LEFT ALIGNMENT:
For modern, technical, editorial and premium designs.

CENTER ALIGNMENT:
For hero compositions, beauty, luxury and minimal designs.

RIGHT ALIGNMENT:
Use only when composition strongly supports it.

Do NOT randomly mix left, center and right alignment.

Text blocks must share invisible alignment lines.

Headlines, subtitles and feature blocks should feel connected to the same grid.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
11. TEXT CONTRAST
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Text must remain readable against the background.

If the background is bright:
use dark typography.

If the background is dark:
use light typography.

If the background is visually complex:
create a subtle clean text zone, gradient separation, translucent panel or controlled contrast area.

DO NOT use outlines simply to compensate for poor composition.

DO NOT place white text on bright areas.

DO NOT place black text on dark areas.

Readability always wins.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
12. COLOR SYSTEM
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Build the color palette from the actual product.

Use:

1 DOMINANT COLOR
2 SUPPORTING COLORS
3 NEUTRAL COLOR
4 OPTIONAL ACCENT COLOR

Maximum approximately 3–4 major colors.

The product itself should influence the palette.

Do not introduce unrelated colors.

Examples:

BLACK PRODUCT:
black / graphite / white / subtle accent.

WHITE PRODUCT:
white / light gray / graphite / subtle accent.

BLUE PRODUCT:
blue / navy / white / neutral gray.

GREEN PRODUCT:
green / dark green / white / neutral.

LUXURY PRODUCT:
neutral palette + one restrained accent.

The palette must feel intentional.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
13. COLOR PSYCHOLOGY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Choose colors according to the product and audience.

TECHNOLOGY:
cool neutrals, graphite, blue, subtle gradients.

LUXURY:
black, ivory, champagne-like neutrals, restrained contrast.

COSMETICS:
soft neutrals, refined tones, elegant contrast.

SPORTS:
strong contrast, energetic accent color.

CHILDREN:
playful colors but controlled composition.

HOME:
warm neutrals and natural tones.

TOOLS:
industrial neutrals, dark tones, functional accent.

FOOD:
natural, appetizing, realistic colors.

Do not force these palettes if the actual product suggests something better.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
14. BACKGROUND
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The background must support the product.

Choose between:

* clean studio;
* premium gradient;
* realistic environment;
* architectural environment;
* lifestyle environment;
* abstract 3D environment;
* minimal editorial background;
* technical environment.

Use the simplest background that communicates the product effectively.

Do NOT create background details that compete with the product.

Avoid:

* meaningless particles;
* random circles;
* random geometric shapes;
* excessive glow;
* excessive fog;
* unnecessary neon;
* decorative clutter.

Every background element must have a visual purpose.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
15. 2D VS 3D DECISION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Do NOT use 3D automatically.

Before adding 3D, ask:

"Does 3D make this product easier to understand or more commercially attractive?"

If NO → use 2D.

If YES → use 3D.

3D is appropriate for:

* dimensional explanations;
* exploded views;
* floating components;
* realistic product environments;
* material visualization;
* depth;
* premium hero scenes;
* technical demonstrations.

2D is appropriate for:

* icons;
* specifications;
* labels;
* diagrams;
* measurements;
* comparison blocks;
* minimal information.

The best result may combine 2D and 3D.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
16. 3D POSITIONING
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

3D elements must have a clear spatial relationship with the product.

Use:

* realistic scale;
* realistic perspective;
* realistic shadows;
* realistic contact points;
* consistent lighting direction.

Do NOT create random floating objects.

If an object floats, there must be a clear visual reason.

All 3D elements must appear to exist in the same physical scene.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
17. ICONS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Use icons only when they improve understanding.

Icons must:

* use one coherent visual style;
* have consistent stroke weight;
* use consistent proportions;
* be simple;
* be immediately understandable.

Do not use generic random icons.

Technical product:
use precise technical symbols.

Lifestyle product:
use elegant minimal icons.

Luxury product:
use very restrained iconography.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
18. FEATURE CALLOUTS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

When explaining product features visually:

Connect the feature to the correct physical part of the product.

Use subtle:

* lines;
* arrows;
* labels;
* circles;
* dimension markers.

The line must clearly point to the relevant feature.

Do not allow callout lines to cross randomly through the product.

Do not place labels so far away that the connection becomes ambiguous.

Callouts should feel like professional industrial/product design graphics.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
19. INFORMATION BLOCKS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

If multiple features are shown, organize them into a clear structure.

Example:

[ICON]
FEATURE TITLE
Short explanation

[ICON]
FEATURE TITLE
Short explanation

[ICON]
FEATURE TITLE
Short explanation

Use consistent:

* spacing;
* typography;
* icon size;
* alignment;
* container size.

Do not create irregular blocks.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
20. VISUAL BALANCE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

After placing all elements, evaluate the composition as a whole.

If the left side is visually heavy:
balance it with whitespace, typography or controlled visual weight.

If the right side is visually heavy:
rebalance the opposite side.

Do NOT fill empty space simply because it exists.

Empty space is intentional.

Premium design often requires significant negative space.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
21. SCALE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The product should generally be the largest physical object in the composition.

Supporting elements must be smaller.

The main headline should be visually dominant among text elements.

Secondary information must remain subordinate.

Never allow a decorative object to become more visually important than the product.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
22. DEPTH
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Create depth using:

* realistic contact shadows;
* foreground/background separation;
* perspective;
* controlled reflections;
* subtle atmospheric perspective;
* layered elements;
* realistic lighting;
* restrained depth of field.

Do NOT blur everything.

The product itself must remain sharp and readable.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
23. LIGHTING
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Use professional commercial product photography lighting.

Lighting direction must be consistent across the entire image.

The product should have:

* realistic highlights;
* physically believable shadows;
* accurate reflections;
* realistic material response;
* clean separation from background.

Choose lighting according to the product.

TECH:
clean studio lighting.

LUXURY:
controlled cinematic lighting.

COSMETICS:
soft beauty lighting.

FOOD:
appetizing commercial lighting.

SPORT:
dynamic directional lighting.

HOME:
natural realistic lighting.

Avoid:

* blown highlights;
* dirty shadows;
* impossible reflections;
* plastic-looking materials;
* inconsistent light directions;
* artificial AI artifacts.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
24. PRODUCT MATERIALS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Metal must look like metal.

Glass must look like glass.

Plastic must look like plastic.

Fabric must look like fabric.

Wood must look like wood.

Leather must look like leather.

Do not make every material glossy.

Do not apply generic "AI shine" to everything.

29. FINAL COMPOSITION TEST
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Before finalizing, mentally reduce the image to a small thumbnail.

Ask:

1. Can I immediately identify the product?
2. Can I immediately understand the main benefit?
3. Is the product the visual hero?
4. Is the headline readable?
5. Is the text hierarchy obvious?
6. Is there enough negative space?
7. Is anything visually unnecessary?
8. Does the background support rather than compete?
9. Are the colors coherent?
10. Does the typography look professional?
11. Are all objects physically believable?
12. Is the product completely accurate?

If any answer is NO, fix the composition before finalizing.
